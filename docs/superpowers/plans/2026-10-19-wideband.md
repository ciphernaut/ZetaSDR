# Wideband Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** View 24 to 50 MHz of spectrum from one radio, run several decoders on it at once at a cost that does not grow with the capture rate, sweep beyond the radio's instantaneous bandwidth with an honest `scan` job, and size the IQ history to the host's memory.

**Architecture:** A polyphase filter bank (PFB) in `zeta-dsp` splits a wide capture into coarse bins once per source; the node owns one bank per wide source (a `ChannelBank` trait in core, a factory registered by `zeta-dsp`, fed like the ring), and the existing `channel` stage draws its input from the nearest bin when the request fits, falling back to the direct filter otherwise, so job graphs do not change. The spectrum stage averages FFTs to a fixed row rate and emits quantised rows so an 8 192-bin waterfall fits the websocket. A `scan` job is a core-run loop that holds the lease on a span, steps the radio across it and emits rows tagged with each step's centre; the UI stitches them. The ring is sized from a memory budget read from `/proc/meminfo`.

**Tech Stack:** Rust 2024 (`zeta-dsp` with `rustfft`, `zeta-core`, `zeta`, `zeta-ui` vanilla JS), Playwright scenarios, the field host's B210 for acceptance.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-18-zetasdr-design.md` sections 3 (footprints and memory), 4.1 (arbitration: "a `scan` job that time-shares", "time-sharing is an explicit job kind"; sample history sized by configuration), 4.2, 4.6 (channeliser on the acceleration ladder; stage implementations with identical semantics), 7 (waterfall).

## Global Constraints

- `zeta-core` gains no dependency: the `ChannelBank` trait and `StageContext` live in core; the PFB lives in `zeta-dsp`.
- Job graphs and op shapes are unchanged except `JobKind::Scan` (a new kind, no new fields): a scan over `center_hz ± bandwidth_hz/2` steps the radio in captures of `min(source max_bandwidth_hz, SCAN_STEP_MAX_HZ = 16_000_000)` with `SCAN_DWELL_MS = 250` per step.
- "There is no silent pre-emption": a scan holds the lease on its whole span; `job_start`/`source_ensure` inside it is a `Conflict` naming the scan job.
- Every `channel` request keeps its semantics: same `center_hz`, `bandwidth_hz`, `out_rate_hz` and provenance whether served by the bank or the direct filter; the output differs only by filter response.
- Cost target: at 24 Msps in a release build, four concurrent `wfm_demod` jobs plus a spectrum job use under one core each on the field host (measured in Task 8), and the bank's own cost is under 20 % of one core.
- Memory: ring seconds per source = `clamp(budget / (rate × 8 B) , 1 s, 10 s)` where the budget defaults to 25 % of `MemTotal` capped at 4 GB; `--ring-budget-mb` overrides; a Pi with 1 GB budget at 2.4 Msps keeps about 10 s, at 16 Msps about 8 s… recomputed on every retune.
- Spectrum rows: `rows_per_s` default 20 at any sample rate; `size` up to 8 192; rows quantised to `u8` dB steps with `floor_db`/`step_db` so a row is at most 8 KB of JSON array (still under the 64 KiB text limit) and the text websocket stays usable; the UI decodes both the old `db` and the new `q` payloads.
- CI runs on synthetic fixtures (no hardware); release-mode timing tests are `#[cfg_attr(debug_assertions, ignore)]` and run in the Task 8 gate with `--release`.
- Gate: `cargo fmt --check`, `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets --all-features -- -D warnings`, `cargo test --workspace --all-features`, `zeta agents-md --check`, `--skill --check`, `cargo test -p zeta --test ui -- --nocapture` with no skip line.

## Review Focus

1. A channel request that straddles two bins or is wider than a bin must still be served correctly (direct path), never with a torn band. Test: Task 2 `channel_wider_than_a_bin_uses_the_direct_path_and_matches`.
2. Stopping one job must not disturb the others sharing the bank (no restart of the bank, no sample gap in the survivors). Test: Task 2 `stopping_one_bank_consumer_leaves_the_others_gapless`.
3. A scan must release the radio when stopped mid-sweep and leave `current` at the last step, never in a half-tuned state, and a decoder started during a scan is refused with the scan named. Test: Task 5 `job_start_during_scan_is_a_conflict_naming_it`, `scan_stop_mid_sweep_releases`.

5. The UI zoom control on a source held by someone else's job must show the conflict, not silently fail or stop their job. Test: Task 6 `zoom_on_held_source_shows_conflict`.

Conventions used by every task:

- **Bank geometry**: `bins = largest power of two with rate / bins ≥ BIN_MIN_HZ (750_000)`, clamped to 8..=256; outputs are 2× oversampled: each bin's stream has `sample_rate_hz = 2 × rate / bins` and `center_hz = source centre + (k − bins/2) × rate / bins`; prototype low-pass cutoff `rate / (2 × bins)`, `8 × bins` Hann-windowed sinc taps. A channel request is served by the bank iff the source rate ≥ `PFB_MIN_RATE_HZ (2_000_000)` and `bandwidth_hz ≤ rate / bins` (one bin width); the nearest bin by centre is used and the existing fine filter runs on that bin's stream. When a bank serves a job, the job's channel `out_rate_hz` is `channel_rate_hz(bin_rate, bandwidth_hz)` (the narrowest exact divisor of the BIN rate, at least 48 kHz and at least the band; the demods resample to 48 kHz anyway); the expansion asks `node.bank(source)` for the bin rate.
- **`ChannelBank` trait (core)**: `fn band(&self) -> Tuning` (the wide capture), `fn bin_hz(&self) -> u64`, `fn subscribe(&self, center_hz: u64, bandwidth_hz: u64) -> Result<(IqReceiver, Hz /*bin rate*/, Hz /*bin centre*/), BankError>` (`BankError::{TooWide{bandwidth_hz, bin_hz}, OutOfBand{..}}`), `fn consumers(&self) -> usize`. `BankFactory`: `fn make(&self, rate_hz: Hz, center_hz: Hz) -> Option<Arc<dyn ChannelBank>>` plus `fn push(&self, block: &IqBlock)` on the bank (fed by the node). `StageContext { source: SourceId, bank: Option<Arc<dyn ChannelBank>> }`; `Stage::run(&self, params, inputs, outputs, bus, cancel, ctx)`.
- **Spectrum event v2**: `{"size", "bin_hz", "rows_per_s", "floor_db", "step_db", "q": [u8 × size], "center_hz"}`; `db = floor_db + q × step_db`; `step_db = 0.5`, `floor_db` = the row's 2nd-percentile minus 5 dB; averaging: power-average every FFT between rows; `size` default for a job = smallest power of two ≥ `bandwidth_hz / 6_000`, clamped 1024..=8192. The UI's column store becomes `Uint8Array` rows.
- **Scan**: `JobKind::Scan` expands to no graph; the node runs `scan_loop`: compute steps from `center ± bw/2` with the step width; for each step `port.tune(step)`, wait `SCAN_DWELL_MS`, run one FFT (size from the step width) on the latest ring samples (≥ 1 FFT's worth), publish a spectrum v2 event with that step's `center_hz` on the job's event stream, advance; wrap around until stopped; the lease band is the full span (`LeaseManager` accepts a band wider than the capture for scan only, flagged `time_shared: true` in `holders` output? keep op shapes: `source_list.current` shows the current step; `holders` lists the scan job). Stop: cancel, leave `current` at the last step.
- **Ring budget**: `NodeConfig.ring_budget_bytes: u64`; `--ring-budget-mb`; default from `/proc/meminfo` `MemTotal` × 0.25 capped at 4 GiB (read in `node_setup`, not core); `IqRing::resize(seconds)` on retune keeps the newest samples.
- **UI band control**: `#band` with centre and width inputs plus a drag on the frequency axis (pan) and wheel (zoom ×2). Zooming and panning INSIDE the current capture is rendering only (the waterfall draws a sub-range of each row's bins; no operation is issued). Only when the requested view leaves the capture does the UI retune: it stops its own spectrum job if that is the only holder, then `source_ensure` for the new band; a `Conflict` (another holder) or `OutOfRange` (a file source cannot retune) is shown in the transcript with the holder ids or the range and the view is reverted. The UI's own spectrum band is the full source capture (`MAX_UI_BANDWIDTH_HZ` removed; the job's `size` follows the band).
